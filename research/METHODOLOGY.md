## Scope and classifications
Energy covers generation, grid delivery, storage and electricity demand. Chips covers accelerators, memory, packaging and manufacturing. Infrastructure covers operational data centers, networking, cooling and construction. Models covers reproducible capability, cost, efficiency and availability. Applications covers adoption and measured practical outcomes.

Records use observation (reported historical measurement), estimate (modeled historical quantity), forecast (future projection), government-target, or company-commitment. Status belongs to each point, not an entire chart. All-data-center measures are contextual indicators, never relabeled as AI-only. Benchmark results remain specific to a benchmark version, model and evaluation protocol; do not generalize one score into universal intelligence.

## Testing the industrial premise

The editorial premise is that abundant power, domestic manufacturing and useful AI can support American reindustrialization. It is a direction for research, not an established national outcome. Trace each supported step from delivered electricity and chip supply to operational AI factories, model and agent reliability, and completed work.

Local value requires local evidence: supplier purchases, wages, realized operating jobs, tax receipts and relevant public costs. Total investment and company sales cannot substitute for these measures. Customer productivity needs a task, comparison, deployment context, quality measure and costs including human oversight where available.

Robotics coverage distinguishes demonstrations, pilots, orders and operational deployments; record task completion, uptime, interventions, physical performance and economics when reported. Medical and drug-discovery coverage distinguishes predictions, laboratory results, trials, approvals and demonstrated patient outcomes. Science, engineering, climate and clean-energy coverage looks for reproducibility and validation beyond a model benchmark or simulation. Preserve negative results and limitations.

Networking coverage includes compute, storage and networking chips, copper and optical links, and the distinction between scale up within systems, scale out across clusters and scale across sites. Record deployed scope, bandwidth and latency in context; do not infer application value from nominal link speed.

The expanded narrative describes research priorities. It does not add observations or imply every topic is covered by the current source registry. New numeric series and sources still require review.

## Period basis for repeated readings · September 9, 2026

A metric without a `period_basis` holds one value per year; a second value for the same year is a conflict that requires a reviewed correction. A metric may instead carry a reviewed `period_basis` of `month` (period `YYYY-MM`), `quarter` (`YYYY-Qn`) or `snapshot` (`YYYY-MM-DD`). Readings are then keyed on the canonical period, so a capacity snapshot, a list price or a weekly service figure can be recorded each time it is disclosed without overwriting history. Historical periods cannot lie in the future, and the period's year must match the record year. Assigning a basis to an existing metric is a reviewed catalog change; the charts currently plot one point per year and need a per-period rendering before a periodic metric is displayed as a series.

## Chip capacity quantification · September 9, 2026

Chips-layer project cards (fabs, packaging sites, HBM plants) previously showed a generic "not quantified" placeholder. `research/chip-capacity.json` is a reviewed configuration of four measurement classes, mirrored to `site/data/chip-capacity.json` by `scripts/chip_capacity.py`, which also creates or updates one project-scoped metric per configured project (id `{project}-capacity`; two projects are deliberately excluded — the TSMC Arizona program envelope, tracked per phase, and GlobalFoundries Singapore, pending a reviewed company entry). The classes:

- **Logic fab** (`wafer_starts_per_month`, wafer starts / month by node, 300 mm equivalent) and **memory fab** (same measurement type, DRAM/NAND) hold monthly wafer-start disclosures.
- **Advanced packaging** (`cowos_or_advanced_packaging_wspm`, CoWoS-class wafer starts / month).
- **HBM packaging** (`hbm_stack_capacity`, HBM stacks / month).

Where no base figure is disclosed, the card states that plainly, names the figure that would quantify the project and links every registered source being watched. Unknown is not zero, and no figure is invented. Where a base figure exists, the card adds an illustrative compute equivalent, always labeled illustrative and never summed across projects:

- **Packaging → accelerators and H100 equivalents.** `chipConversions.impliedRatios(observations)` (`site/assets/chip-conversions.js`) finds the latest quarter where Nvidia's CoWoS-wafer consumption (`epoch-nvidia-cowos-wafers-quarterly`) and both Nvidia cumulative series (`epoch-nvidia-ai-chips-cumulative`, `epoch-nvidia-ai-compute-cumulative`) also carry the previous quarter, so a quarterly shipment figure can be derived from the cumulative difference. It returns `acceleratorsPerCowosWafer` and `h100ePerAccelerator` for that quarter, plus the latest full-year shipment figure from `epoch-nvidia-ai-chips-cumulative-yearly` (year-end cumulative difference). `packagingEquivalent(wspm, ratios)` = wafer starts/month × 12 × `acceleratorsPerCowosWafer` for accelerators/year, then × `h100ePerAccelerator` for H100e/year. These are Epoch AI's Nvidia-mix ratios applied to a different site's disclosed wafer starts — a scale illustration, not a forecast or a company disclosure for that site.
- **Logic fab → an H100-class die ceiling.** `grossDiePerWafer(areaMm2, diameterMm)` = ⌊π(d/2)²/A − πd/√(2A)⌋, the standard reticle-die approximation, using the Nvidia GH100 (H100) reference die (814 mm², 300 mm wafer, `nvidia-hopper-whitepaper`). `logicCeiling(wspm, diePerWafer)` = wafer starts/month × 12 × die/wafer. This is a ceiling: perfect yield, full allocation to one reticle-scale accelerator die. Actual product mix and yields are undisclosed and are not this figure. Where the project's company is TSMC, the card also shows that annualized wafer-start figure as a share of TSMC's company-wide `tsmc-wafer-capacity` (million 12-inch-equivalent wafers/year).
- **Memory fab and HBM packaging → no conversion.** Die size, stack configuration and yields per generation are undisclosed, so a stacks/month or DRAM wafer-starts figure cannot be translated into accelerator or H100e counts here. The card shows the base figure only, with a link to Epoch's separate value-basis HBM series (`epoch-hbm-supply-quarterly`).

The conversion functions are pure and DOM-free (no globals, no fetch), unit-tested under `node:test` in `tests/chip_conversions.cjs`, and exercised against the real ledger data and end to end (including refusing an unknown configured project or an unregistered source) in `tests/test_chip_capacity.py`. `scripts/validate_chip_capacity.py` requires the mirrored file to match the reviewed configuration and every configured project and source to be reviewed, following the same file-validation pattern as `validate_delivery.py`.

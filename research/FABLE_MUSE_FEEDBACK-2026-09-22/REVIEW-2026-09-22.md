## What went wrong

The package treats Amazon's two figures as two sources disagreeing about one fact. They are two statements made at different times:

- **February 5, 2026**, Amazon's fourth-quarter release: "we expect to invest about $200 billion in capital expenditures across Amazon in 2026".
- **July 30, 2026**, the second-quarter call, as AP reported it: Andy Jassy "disclosed to investors on the call that Amazon now expects capital spending to total $220 billion", which AP calls "up from the $200 billion investment plan that was announced in February".

Amazon's July release does not restate the figure; it was given on the call. So the current guidance has only a secondary written source, and that is fine: a later statement supersedes an earlier one whatever the channel. Preferring the issuer's own channel is right when two sources describe **the same statement**. It is wrong when they describe **different dates**, because then the older primary is simply out of date.

The gap fill applied the rule the package teaches, replaced $220B with $200B on the live site, and understated Amazon's current guidance by $20B. That was reverted in commit a6a121c36. Applied to the model brief, the same rule would make the nightly model discard the current figure every time it saw it.

## Corrected drop-in text

Use these in place of the CapEx wording in both patches. Everything else in the patches can be applied as proposed.

**Model brief, "Distinctions that must survive extraction"** (replaces the sentence beginning "CapEx guidance is not cash CapEx"):

> CapEx guidance is not cash CapEx already spent and not commissioned capacity. When two sources give different figures, first check whether they report the same statement or statements made on different dates. For the same statement, prefer the issuer's own IR release, official earnings transcript or SEC exhibit over a news or third-party rewrite. For different dates, the later statement is the current guidance whatever its channel, and the earlier one is an older vintage, not a conflict; record the later one attributed to whoever reported it, and name the primary transcript as the evidence that would settle it.

**Model brief, "Screening rules for reviewer passes"** (replaces the second and third sentences of the proposed paragraph):

> For `capital-guidance-*` metrics, a figure whose only support is a news report of an earnings call passes attribution when the note names the reporter, the call and its date; flag it for primary follow-up. It fails when an issuer document from the same date or later gives a different figure. A large-load queue number offered for a delivered-load, generation-queue or operating-IT metric fails scope_matches.

**Model brief, worked examples** (replaces the "$220B vs about $200B" row):

> | February IR release: "about $200 billion" for the year. July call, reported by a wire service: now "$220 billion", up from February's plan | The July figure is current guidance: record it attributed to the wire report of the call, status forecast, note the February plan it supersedes and that a primary transcript would settle it. The February figure is an older vintage, not a conflicting source. |

**Operating guide, the new ISO/RTO and CapEx section** (replaces its second paragraph):

> Hyperscaler CapEx guidance for `capital-guidance-*` metrics should come from the issuer's IR release, official earnings-event transcript page or SEC exhibit when one states the figure. Guidance given only on a call can be recorded from a credible report of that call, attributed to the reporter with the call's date, and marked for primary follow-up. Compare the dates of the statements before comparing the rank of the sources: a later statement supersedes an earlier one.

**Operating guide, teaching table** (replaces the "Secondary earnings transcript or Fool/AP CapEx figure" row):

> | Secondary report of a CapEx figure vs the issuer's IR release | Same statement: prefer the issuer. Different dates: the later one is current, the earlier an older vintage. A call-only figure may be recorded from a credible report of the call, attributed, with primary follow-up. |

**Eval fixture** (replaces the Amazon case in `BACKEND_COLLECTION_NOTES.md`): Amazon's February release ("about $200 billion") plus a July wire report of the call ("$220 billion, up from the $200 billion plan announced in February"). The expected answer records $220B as current, attributed to the wire report, with the February plan noted as superseded. A model that rejects the $220B fails this case.

## Why neither patch was applied on 09/22

- `research/runtime.json` sets `"instructions": "brief"`, so `MODEL_BRIEF.md` goes straight into the nightly model's extraction and screening prompts. An edit takes effect at the next run.
- `research/MUSE_RESEARCH_PLAYBOOK.md`: "After prompt/model changes, review calibration results before recommending a long publishing run." The nightly is one.
- Calibration means running the model (`scripts/evaluate_extraction.py --holdout`) on the local Ollama the agents share. `AGENTS.md` does not let a maintainer start inference to check documentation, so the owner starts it.
- The package itself says the additions should bump `metric_rules_version` and `note_rules_version`, which changes the processing identity and reprocesses saved documents. That should follow a measured result, not precede it.
